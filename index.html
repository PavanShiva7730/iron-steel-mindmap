Iron and Steel Industry in India
History and Evolution
Ancient Period (700 BC)
Produced wrought iron
Traditional methods (Karya furnace)
Medium carbon content
Medieval Period (400 AD)
Iron Pillar of Delhi example
Crucible-worked wrought iron
British Rule / Industrialization
First modern blast furnace (Calcutta, 1830s)
First successful production (1890s)
Rapid growth late 19th century
Modern Integrated Steel
TISCO/Tata Steel incorporated (1907)
Indian Iron and Steel Co. & Mysore Steel Works
High costs, poor quality, import dependence
Post Independence (Self-Reliance)
Government active role
Public sector enterprises with Soviet aid
Plants: Bokaro, Bhilai, Rourkela, Durgapur
Liberalization (1990s)
Entry of private sector (Essar, JSW, Tata)
Current: One of the largest global producers
Key Features and Growth
Large Scale Production
2nd largest crude steel producer
Meets 95% of domestic steel needs
Employment
2 million direct & indirect jobs
Sector Dominance
Public sector (SAIL, RINL)
Growing private firms (Tata, JSW, Essar)
Raw Material Advantage
10% of world’s iron ore reserves
5th largest coal reserves
Innovation Focus
Developing specialized steel grades
Cheaper raw material availability (but regulation challenges)
Major Challenges
High Production Costs
Outdated technology
High logistics expenses
Import Dependence
30% of total steel needs imported
Need for high-grade/special varieties
Infrastructure and Logistics
Poor transport networks
Bottlenecks, traffic jams, delays
Technology and Efficiency
Uses suboptimal processes
Lower energy efficiency
Environmental and Safety Issues
High pollution (dust, gases, heat)
Poor worker safety records
Raw Material Shortages
Depletion of reserves
Delays in mining permissions
Weak Branding & Marketing
Forces higher-cost imports
